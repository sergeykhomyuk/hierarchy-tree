## Answers to the six attacks

**1. Domain rules.** The rules survive the obvious attacks: because a user has at most one manager (GOAL.md), the graph is functional, so cycles are disjoint, invariant 10's "earliest ring member" is always unique, and invariant 12's termination is structural under TECH's colour walk. Self-management (9) is a ring of length 1 handled consistently by 10. Duplicate ids (13) + dangling (8) compose cleanly. What breaks: invariant 95's "N hidden" against 18/28/36 (finding 3), the people-count divergence when 13 fires (finding 22), and the array-envelope assumption (finding 7). One softer ambiguity I did not raise as its own finding: invariant 13's "first occurrence in payload order" is relative to the *validated* list, not the raw payload, since invariant 42 drops invalid rows first — so `[{id:1, firstName:123}, {id:1, valid}]` keeps the second occurrence. It has no behavioural consequence on the live data but the wording should say which list. Invariant 18 vs 95: they mean the same number only under the "direct reports" reading of "N hidden"; the mockup does not discriminate.

**2. Contradictions TECH did not self-report.** Invariant 153 vs 175 vs ARCHITECTURE §2 (finding 10). Invariant 65 vs the kit's frozen `ErrorState` (finding 5). Invariants 89/151 vs the kit's frozen `Avatar` (finding 13). TECH's "only phase-2 surface" vs its own `NavigationRail` (finding 17). Invariant 47 requires branded **emails** ("a raw number **or a raw string**") and ARCHITECTURE §4 agrees; TECH §1 declares `personIdentifier.ts` and a `Person` whose `email` is a plain string, with no `emailAddress.ts` — an unreported gap I fold here rather than pad the list. ARCHITECTURE §4's "container queries on tree rows so deep nesting stays usable on narrow screens" appears nowhere in either spec, which is the mechanism invariant 96 would need.

**3. Untestable invariants.** 43 — false and unfalsifiable (finding 14); the testable form is 159's. 96 — unbounded viewport quantifier, no check exists (finding 27). 84 — no observable criterion *and* not satisfied by the stated mechanism (finding 20). 102 — "immediate" has no threshold; the falsifiable half ("no request") is falsified by finding 1. 104 — actually testable (`window.scrollY` before/after in Playwright), and safe here because the app renders no `<ScrollRestoration>`, so a push does not reset scroll; no finding. 161 — testable by spying on `buildForest`, and violated by finding 1. 164 — names a budget that does not exist (finding 27). 137 is testable but weaker than it reads: axe will not catch finding 25.

**4. Feasibility against real code.** TECH's phase-2 and phase-1 citations are accurate except `buildContentSecurityPolicy.ts:14` (the `img-src` line is 16) and "`createRuntime` wires `correlationId` to `interactionTracker.currentCorrelationId()`" (it is `currentCorrelationId() ?? createCorrelationId(randomness)`, `createRuntime.ts:54-56`). The boundaries policy does permit what TECH proposes for imports (`feature → shared|platform` only, `eslint.config.js:348-368`) — but it forbids `feature → app`, which is what strands the Clock and the observability facade (finding 8). The repository's own guard scripts **do not** permit TECH's names or markup (finding 2). react-router does support the object-holding-a-promise loader pattern and `useSearchParams` push-by-default, but push-per-toggle triggers default revalidation (finding 1) and `useRevalidator` does not open a new interaction (finding 6). The correlation-id path is real (`createHttpClient.ts:76`, `recordTiming(... correlationId ...)`), and phase 2's caller-passes-the-id pattern is real.
